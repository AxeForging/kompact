# Impeccable

## Design Context

### Users

Engineers deciding whether to install a context-compaction plugin, plus
AxeForging teammates and people arriving from the two upstream projects. They
read this on a laptop, mid-workday, already sceptical: they have seen a hundred
dev-tool pages claiming "10x faster" and they discount all of them.

The job to be done is narrow and fast: **decide in about a minute whether the
measurements are credible and the thing is worth installing.** Not "be
inspired". Not "learn about compaction". Judge the evidence.

### Brand Personality

**Measured, unsentimental, forthright.**

This is a product whose entire thesis is honest measurement. Its headline
finding is that a 322M-parameter neural model *lost* to thirteen coefficients.
It publishes a corrected number (0.918 was a single split; repeated, 0.895 ±
0.072) and a verification table with an explicit "not verified" row. Confidence
comes from showing the working, including the parts that went against us.

The interface must therefore feel like a result, not a pitch. If it looks like
it is overselling, it contradicts the product.

### Aesthetic Direction

A typeset technical document: a calibration certificate, a standards paper, a
lab report someone took seriously. Data-forward and typographic. Confident
through precision and restraint, not through effects.

Light theme, deliberately. The context is daytime evaluation on a laptop, and
the paper-report metaphor wants paper. It is also a deliberate refusal of the
dev-tool reflex (dark background, neon accent, glow) which here would read as
exactly the overselling the product argues against.

**Anti-references:** SaaS hero with a gradient headline and three identical
feature cards. Purple-to-blue gradients. Glowing dark mode. Big round icons
above every heading. Any "10x" claim without an interval next to it.

**Differentiation:** the centrepiece is the measurement itself, presented as a
real result — error bars, worst case, and the negative findings given the same
typographic weight as the wins. The thing a visitor remembers is that the page
told them where the product loses.

### Design Principles

1. **Every number carries its uncertainty.** A figure without its spread, its
   worst case, or its n is not allowed on the page.
2. **Negative results get equal weight.** The silent-truncation finding, the
   0.002 near-tie and the unverified row are content, not footnotes.
3. **Typography does the work.** Hierarchy from scale, weight and space — not
   from cards, borders, shadows or colour fills.
4. **Colour is evidence, not decoration.** One accent, reserved for measured
   values and honesty markers. If it appears anywhere else, it is wrong.
5. **Earn the install in one screen.** What it is, what it measured, what it
   costs, how to install — legible without scrolling on a laptop.
