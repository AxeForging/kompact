# Impeccable

## Design Context

### Users

Engineers deciding whether to install a context-compaction plugin, plus
AxeForging teammates and people arriving from the two upstream projects. They
read this on a laptop, mid-workday, already sceptical: they have seen a hundred
dev-tool pages claiming "10x faster" and they discount all of them.

The job to be done is narrow and fast: **decide in about a minute whether the
measurements are credible and the thing is worth installing.** Not "be
inspired". Not "learn about compaction". Judge the evidence.

### Brand Personality

**Measured, unsentimental, forthright.**

This is a product whose entire thesis is honest measurement. Its headline
finding is that a 322M-parameter neural model *lost* to thirteen coefficients.
It publishes a corrected number (0.918 was a single split; repeated on a wider corpus, 0.905 ±
0.078, and 0.789 leave-one-out across all 41 sessions) and a verification table with an explicit "not verified" row. Confidence
comes from showing the working, including the parts that went against us.

The interface must therefore feel like a result, not a pitch. If it looks like
it is overselling, it contradicts the product.

### Aesthetic Direction

A typeset technical document: a calibration certificate, a standards paper, a
lab report someone took seriously. Data-forward and typographic. Confident
through precision and restraint, not through effects.

**Revised after the first release — visually.** The document was right and too
quiet; it read as a lab report that had not decided whether it wanted to be read.
Two references were taken, each only for what it is good at.

TypeSafe's Jev launch post supplies the **structure**: evidence, then a
demonstration, then the evals, then what is next, then an FAQ that asks the
sceptic's questions out loud.

Nous's Hermes page supplies the **visual language, and nothing else**: a flooded
saturated ground rather than an accent on paper, a high-contrast display serif at
real scale, and one arresting image carrying the page. What is deliberately *not*
taken is its voice. Hermes is a manifesto with no numbers on it. This product's
whole argument is that it does not oversell, so the copy, the structure and every
claim stay as the rest of this file describes them — measured, unsentimental,
forthright, every figure carrying its spread.

The one-line test: **it should look like Hermes and read like a lab report.** If
a sentence gets louder to match the new ground, the ground was applied wrongly.
The figures went *down* this pass — leave-one-out AUC 0.789 where an earlier,
narrower corpus said 0.862 — and the page says so in the same breath as the
colour.

Light theme, deliberately. The context is daytime evaluation on a laptop, and
the paper-report metaphor wants paper. It is also a deliberate refusal of the
dev-tool reflex (dark background, neon accent, glow) which here would read as
exactly the overselling the product argues against.

**Anti-references:** SaaS hero with a gradient headline and three identical
feature cards. Purple-to-blue gradients. Glowing dark mode. Big round icons
above every heading. Any "10x" claim without an interval next to it.

**Differentiation:** the centrepiece is the measurement itself, presented as a
real result — error bars, worst case, and the negative findings given the same
typographic weight as the wins. The thing a visitor remembers is that the page
told them where the product loses.

### Design Principles

1. **Every number carries its uncertainty.** A figure without its spread, its
   worst case, or its n is not allowed on the page.
2. **Negative results get equal weight.** The silent-truncation finding, the
   0.002 near-tie and the unverified row are content, not footnotes.
3. **Typography does the work.** Hierarchy from scale, weight and space — not
   from cards, borders, shadows or colour fills.
4. **Colour is evidence, not decoration.** One accent, reserved for measured
   values and honesty markers. If it appears anywhere else, it is wrong.
5. **Earn the install in one screen.** What it is, what it measured, what it
   costs, how to install — legible without scrolling on a laptop.
6. **Motion must carry information.** One exception to the old no-animation
   rule, and it is a narrow one: the demonstration steps through real decisions
   in the order the scorer makes them, so the reader *sees* the ranking work and
   the transcript shrink. A number that ticks upward for effect is still banned;
   a number that ticks upward because the reader is watching characters actually
   being freed is the content.
7. **Show the thing running.** The page argues from a table; a sceptic wants to
   watch it decide. The demonstration is a constructed session with real
   decisions — the shipped scorer, the shipped defaults, nothing chosen by hand —
   and it says so on its face.

## Detector findings that are the detector's own

`npx impeccable` reports four things on these pages that are not true of them.
Each was isolated by bisection and mutation rather than waved away, and the
first, third and fourth share one root cause: **the detector cannot resolve
`clamp()` font sizes**, so every fluid step is divided by a bogus figure.

- **`tight-leading` ×2 on `index.html`** ("0.14x", "0.13x"). Every declared
  `line-height` in the file is between 1.0 and 1.75, and every computed
  `lineHeight/fontSize` in Chromium is in `{1.0, 1.06, 1.1, 1.15, 1.35, 1.45,
  1.5, 1.6, 1.62, 1.75}`. The two findings bisect to `.standfirst` (1.45) and
  `.finding p` (1.35), both above the detector's own 1.3 threshold. Replacing
  `--text-lg`'s `clamp()` with the literal it resolves to above 1113px — zero
  rendered difference — makes both vanish.
- **`flat-type-hierarchy`** (bundled 2.1.1 detector). Same cause: it sees
  `12–21.6px` and misses every `clamp()`-derived step. The rendered scale at
  1280px is `12, 14, 16, 17, 18.4, 21.6, 25.6, 30.4, 65.6, 112` — 9.3×.
- **`cramped-padding` on `section.flooded`** ("children flush against
  border-top+bg on all sides, no inset"). It computes `70.4px 0` of block
  padding at 1280px with `64px` of inline inset on the inner `.wrap`, and
  `section.flooded` sets `border-top: none`, so the border it names does not
  exist.
- **`em-dash-overuse` on `eval.html`** ("182 em-dashes in body text"). 175 of
  the 182 are ASCII hyphen runs inside `<pre>` — five horizontal rules in
  generated console output. The file contains 15 real em-dashes, 8 of them in
  visible prose.

Anything else the detector reports is a real finding until proved otherwise.
